The 60-second catch-up

Everything that matters, in one paragraph. Rewritten every update.

As of Sunday, Nov 22 After gangs ambushed four Salvadoran aviation technicians outside Port-au-Prince on Oct 18, Nayib Bukele sent roughly 2,500 troops to Haiti, operating apart from the UN-backed Gang Suppression Force. They've helped retake the national stadium (Nov 12), which is now a forward base, and Haitian forces launched the first major push in five years into Martissant, the gang-held chokepoint on the road south (Nov 19). The mission has its first deaths: three Salvadoran soldiers were killed in an explosion on Nov 20. The same day, Haiti announced a deal for a Salvadoran company to help build three new prisons. The four original missing men are still unaccounted for. Thousands of Haitians have marched in support, while the UN warns civilians must be protected and Bukele, now running for a third term at home, is feuding with a US diplomat over coordination.

      What to watch next

      The open questions that will drive the next headlines.

      The four missing techniciansThe 400 Mawozo-linked gang Lanmò San Jou claims all four are dead. Bukele says remains recovered on Oct 24 aren't theirs. Proof either way would be a turning point.
      Does Martissant hold?Clearing Route 2 would reconnect Port-au-Prince to the south and west for the first time in five years.
      Washington vs. BukeleBukele has publicly blasted a US diplomat over coordination concerns, even though US Southern Command flew his troops in.
      Two electionsHaiti has a 33-candidate presidential race. Bukele faces voters on Feb 28, 2027, and the Haiti mission is now part of his campaign.

      The debate

      The strongest version of each side's case.

      Supporters say

      • Results are visible: a stadium held by gangs since early 2024 was retaken in weeks.
      • Haitians themselves are asking for it. Thousands marched on Nov 17 to praise the troops.
      • The UN-backed force alone hadn't turned the tide, and more than 1,000 people were killed in a single quarter.

      Critics say

      • The UN's human rights office warns operations must put civilian protection first amid "atrocious abuses."
      • Experts warn Haiti has no long-term plan, and its own police are underpaid (about $300/month for junior officers).
      • Bukele's record at home, including mass trials and a third-term bid after rewriting the rules, raises accountability concerns.

      Methodology & corrections

      How this tracker is built, so you can judge it for yourself.

      Sourcing standardEvery event and number links to a published report from an established outlet or official body (AP, Reuters, the UN, the Haitian National Police and others). The green badge on each item opens its source.
      Disputed claimsWhen parties disagree, as on the fate of the four missing technicians, both claims are shown and attributed. Nothing is reported as fact until it's confirmed.
      IndependenceHaiti Frontline isn't affiliated with any government, military, party or the Gang Suppression Force. Tips from readers don't influence coverage.
      CorrectionsErrors are fixed promptly, and significant corrections are noted in the timeline with the date they were made. Spot one? Reply to the tracker on X.
